Front matter

What this is and what has been decided

0.1 Objective & scope

Build a single platform that runs the company's freight operation — booking through settlement — with a permanent, auditable record of custody for every consignment.

Three shipment modes are in scope from day one, because they use the same spine but diverge sharply in the middle of the journey:

ModeUnit soldHub handlingManifestRating basis
FTL — Full Truck LoadOne vehicle, point to pointNone (direct)Not required; the trip is the documentPer trip / per km / vehicle type
PTL — Part LoadWeight & volume on a shared vehicleSort, consolidate, transshipRequired per trip legChargeable weight × lane rate
Courier / parcelIndividual packagesFull sort, bin allocationRequired, high line countSlab rate + surcharges

Out of scope for this document: warehousing-as-a-service (3PL inventory ownership), international freight forwarding, customs, and air/rail multimodal. The data model leaves room for them but no requirement here assumes them.

Part A

Business requirements

A.1 The operating thesis

The failure mode to design against is a system where an operator opens a shipment and types the new status into a dropdown. That produces a tracking board that is always slightly fictional, and it answers no dispute.

Instead: operators perform operations, and status is a consequence. A hub clerk scans a package into the dock — the system now knows the shipment arrived, at which branch, at what minute, by whose hands. A dispatcher loads that package onto a manifest — the system now knows which vehicle is carrying it. The vehicle's GPS moves — the system knows where the goods physically are. A delivery agent captures an OTP and a photograph — the system knows custody transferred and can prove it.

Three consequences follow, and they shape most of Part B:

  • Events are append-only. A correction is a new compensating event with a reason code, never an edit. The history in §A.11 is therefore always complete.
  • Every event carries who, where, when, and on what device. That is the chain of custody, and it is what settles a damage claim six months later.
  • Offline capture is safe. Because events are additive and idempotent, a delivery agent in a basement can queue five scans and sync them an hour later without corrupting anything.

A.2 Shipment lifecycle

Every consignment carries one LR/AWB number — CL202608250001 — and moves through the track below. The right-hand column names what actually causes the transition, because that is the requirement; the status itself is just a label.

Booked BOOKED
Booking saved by an executive, a customer in the portal, a bulk upload, or the API. LR number issued.
Pickup Assigned PICKUP_ASSIGNED
Ops assigns executive + vehicle + slot. Task appears in the field PWA.
Picked Up PICKED_UP
Field confirmation: package count, weight, photo, signature or OTP. Discrepancies against booking are recorded, not silently overwritten.
Received at Origin RECEIVED_AT_ORIGIN
Inbound scan at the origin branch. First formal custody transfer to the network.
Processed PROCESSED
Weighed, measured, labelled, routed, binned. Chargeable weight is finalised here — this is the number that bills.
Manifest Created MANIFESTED
Added to a manifest for a specific trip leg. PTL and courier only; FTL skips straight to loading.
Dispatched DISPATCHED
Loading sheet closed, vehicle gate-out recorded, trip started.
In Transit IN_TRANSIT automatic
Set by the system once the vehicle leaves the origin geofence, or on a timer if no GPS is attached.
Arrived at Hub ARRIVED_AT_HUB automatic
Geofence entry at the destination or transshipment hub. Manual fallback for non-GPS vehicles.
Received at Hub RECEIVED_AT_HUB
Physical inbound scan against the manifest. Short, excess, or damaged packages raise exceptions here automatically (§A.10).
Assigned for Delivery ASSIGNED_FOR_DELIVERY
Attached to a delivery run for an agent, with route sequence and COD list.
Out for Delivery OUT_FOR_DELIVERY
Agent starts the run and scans the packages out. Custody now sits with a named person.
Delivered DELIVERED
OTP verified or signature captured, photo taken, GPS stamped, COD recorded if applicable.
POD Uploaded POD_UPLOADED automatic
Fires when signature and photo assets finish syncing — which may be minutes after delivery on a weak connection.
Closed CLOSED automatic
POD complete, COD reconciled, no open exception. The shipment leaves the operational working set.

Exception states run alongside, not instead

A failed delivery attempt must not destroy the main status. If DELIVERY_ATTEMPTED overwrote OUT_FOR_DELIVERY, you lose the fact that the shipment is still out with an agent. The model is therefore one main status plus zero or more open exceptions plus counters (attemptCount, holdReason).

Design rule

Events carry both occurredAt (device clock at the moment of the act) and recordedAt (server receipt). They differ by hours when a field device was offline. Every timeline sorts on occurredAt; every SLA calculation and dispute reads both.

Bulk upload

Corporate customers upload CSV/XLSX. Flow: upload → parse → validate row by row → show a marked-up grid with errors in place → fix inline or re-upload → confirm → create. Validation covers PIN serviceability, duplicate customer reference, weight/dimension sanity, rate-card coverage for the lane, and COD limits. Partial commit is allowed: valid rows book, invalid rows stay in the staging table for correction. Every upload keeps its source file for audit.

India-specific — do not skip

The LR is a consignment note under GST, which makes the service a GTA supply and usually puts it under reverse charge. Booking must capture consignor and consignee GSTINs and who bears the tax, because that decision changes the invoice. Separately, goods above the state threshold need an e-way bill, and Part-B carries the vehicle number — which changes at every transshipment. Both belong in the booking and dispatch screens from Phase 2, not bolted on later.

A.5 Pickup

Two triggers: a booking that needs collection, or a standalone pickup request raised by a customer in the portal before they know what they are shipping.

  • Assignment. Ops picks executive, vehicle, date, slot, and priority. The screen shows each executive's current load and area so assignment is informed rather than blind.
  • Field task. The PWA lists today's pickups in sequence with customer, address, contact, expected packages, and one-tap navigate and call.
  • Confirmation. Actual package count and weight are captured against expected. A mismatch does not silently overwrite the booking — it records both figures and flags the difference for the branch to reconcile, because that difference is money.
  • Failure. Reason code from a configurable list, plus photo and remarks. The system auto-creates a re-pickup task per the branch's retry policy.
  • Bulk pickup. One visit collecting many shipments produces one pickup event per shipment but a single visit record, so executive productivity reports are honest.

A.6 Hub & branch operations

The hub screen is a scanning console, not a form. Everything is driven by a barcode gun or the phone camera, and the sequence is receive → scan → verify → weigh → sort → label → route.

FunctionWhat it does

WeighmentActual and volumetric capture, with weighbridge integration where available. Revised chargeable weight flows to billing and notifies the customer if it exceeds booked weight by more than a configured tolerance.
Sorting & binsScan assigns a destination bin by route. Bin occupancy is visible so the floor knows what is building up for which lane.
LabellingReprint LR labels, package labels, and route stickers to a thermal printer.

Hub-to-hub chain

Faridabad → Delhi → Jaipur → Ahmedabad → delivery branch is a chain of legs, each with its own manifest, trip, and inbound scan. The shipment timeline shows the full chain; the customer sees a simplified version. Because each leg is a separate record, transit time per leg is measurable — which is how you find the hub that is actually costing you your SLA.

A.7 Part load vs full truck load

These two share the booking screen and the event log and diverge everywhere in between. Modelling them as one flow with flags produces a system that does neither well.

PTL — manifest

A manifest is the document for one trip leg. It carries manifest number, vehicle, driver, origin, destination, route, departure and expected arrival, seal number, and the shipment lines. The system totals packages, actual weight, chargeable weight, and volume, and shows capacity utilisation against the vehicle so a dispatcher can see they are sending out a half-empty truck before it leaves.

FTL — trip

Flow: booking → vehicle type and capacity confirmed → own vehicle or vendor assigned → driver assigned → vehicle reporting at loading point → loading → loading confirmation with photos and seal → dispatch → GPS tracking → unloading → POD → trip closure with expenses and settlement. Freight is negotiated per trip, so the FTL booking screen carries a rate-confirmation block with customer freight, vendor freight, and the margin visible to authorised roles only.

A.9 GPS, geofencing, ETA

Tracking is a layer over the operation, deliberately isolated so a GPS provider can be swapped or a second one added without touching shipment logic.

  • Live map. All active vehicles, coloured by status and delay. Click a vehicle for current location, speed, last ping age, distance covered, distance remaining, ETA, driver, trip, and the shipments on board.
  • Geofences. Circular or polygon fences around every hub, branch, warehouse, large customer site, and delivery zone. Entry and exit generate events automatically — which removes most manual arrival and departure updates and is the single highest-value automation in the system.
  • Route deviation. Planned polyline per lane; sustained departure beyond a threshold raises an exception with the deviation point on a map.
  • Stoppage detection. Stationary beyond a configured duration outside a known fence raises a stoppage alert with duration and location.
  • Signal loss. No ping for N minutes raises a NO_GPS_UPDATE exception — the absence of data is itself an operational signal.
  • Trip replay. Historical route playback with speed profile, for disputes and driver review.
Fallback, always

Attached and vendor vehicles will not all have working GPS. Every automatic event must have a manual equivalent with the same permission and audit treatment, and reports must distinguish system-generated from manually-entered arrivals. A system that only works with perfect telematics will not survive contact with a mixed fleet.

A.10 Last mile, POD, and attempts

Delivery run

The branch builds a run: agent, vehicle, date, and an ordered list of stops. Sequencing is manual with an optional optimise button in Phase 8. Each run carries a COD total the agent is accountable for at day end.

Agent PWA

Today's list with shipment, consignee, address, phone, package count, COD amount, and priority. Actions: navigate, call (through a masked number where privacy matters), start delivery, verify OTP, collect payment, capture signature, capture photo, mark delivered, or record a failed attempt.

Attempts are a history, not a boolean

Marking "not delivered" without a reason destroys the information you need. Every attempt writes a permanent row: attempt number, timestamp, GPS, agent, reason code, remarks, photo. Attempt 1 "consignee not available" and attempt 2 "delivered" both survive forever, and the first-attempt-success rate — one of the most important numbers in last-mile logistics — becomes measurable.

Reason codes are master data — operations can add, retire, and change the auto-action without a release.

Proof of delivery

A POD record binds: receiver name and relationship to consignee, signature, delivery photograph, package photograph where required, timestamp, GPS coordinates, OTP reference, remarks, agent, and shipment. It renders to a branded PDF that the customer can download from the portal and that can be attached to an invoice. Assets upload in the background — a weak signal delays the PDF, never the delivery confirmation.

COD

Five states, each a ledger row: expected → collected → deposited → reconciled → settled. The agent's day-end screen shows collected versus deposited with the shortfall in red. Cash deposits post against a branch cash account; digital collections reconcile against the gateway settlement file. Remittance to the customer runs on their contracted cycle, with a remittance advice listing every shipment in the batch.

SLA engine

SLA is defined per service type and origin–destination zone pair — for example Delhi → Jaipur, express, 24 hours. Transit clocks run on a working calendar with branch holidays and cut-off times, so a booking after the evening cut-off starts its clock the next working morning. The scanner runs continuously, marks at-risk and breached shipments, and stamps the reason for breach where it can infer one from the event log (late dispatch, hub dwell, failed attempt). The dashboard reports on time, delayed, breached, and pending as counts and percentages by lane, branch, and customer.

Complaints

Categories: delay, damage, missing, wrong delivery, billing, POD, pickup, behaviour, other. Workflow: Open → Assigned → Investigation → Action → Resolved → Closed, with a per-category response and resolution SLA, a full message thread visible to the customer, and a reopen path.

A.12 Rating, billing, and receivables

Rate cards

A rate card belongs to a customer (or to a default published tariff) and is versioned with effective-from and effective-to dates, so historical invoices always reprice to what was agreed at the time. Lookup dimensions: customer, service type, mode, origin zone or city, destination zone or city, weight slab, vehicle type for FTL, and volume.

Invoicing

Bill runs are generated per customer for a period or per shipment on delivery, depending on the contract. Each invoice line traces back to its shipment and to the stored calculation trace, so "why is this ₹4,280?" is answerable in one click. Supported: consolidated monthly invoices, branch-wise invoices, credit notes, debit notes for revised weight, and re-issue with a full amendment trail.

Receivables

Customer ledger, ageing buckets (0–30/31–60/61–90/90+), credit limit with a booking block when exceeded, payment recording with allocation across invoices, part payments, TDS deduction, and a customer statement of account. Outstanding is visible to the customer in the portal.

A.13 Vendors, trip expenses, settlement

  • Vendor master. Transporter, broker, or attached-vehicle owner, with PAN, GSTIN, bank details, service lanes, and rate contract.
  • Vendor rate contract. Lane-wise or vehicle-type-wise payable rates, mirroring the customer rate card structure so margin per trip is computable.
  • Trip expenses. Diesel, toll, parking, loading, unloading, driver allowance, repair, police/miscellaneous, each with amount, date, payment mode, bill photo, and approval state. Every expense binds to trip, vehicle, driver, and vendor.
  • Advances. Cash or fuel-card advances to driver or vendor before or during a trip, tracked against the settlement.
  • Driver settlement. Trip earning less advances, less approved expenses, less deductions (damage, shortage, fines), equals net payable, with an approval step before payout.
  • Vendor bill and payable. Vendor raises or the system generates a payable per trip; reconciliation against the rate contract flags variances before payment.
  • Profitability. Per trip and per shipment: revenue less vendor freight less trip expenses less allocated overhead.

A.14 Customer portal & public tracking

Public tracking needs no login: enter an LR or customer reference number and see a clean milestone list — Booked, Picked Up, Dispatched, In Transit, Reached destination city, Out for Delivery, Delivered — with dates and city names only. Internal branch names, vehicle numbers, driver identities, cost data, and internal exception notes are never exposed. Multi-shipment lookup and a shareable tracking link are included.

Logged-in portal for corporate customers: create single and bulk bookings, request pickups, track everything, download POD PDFs and invoices, view outstanding and ageing, download shipment and performance reports, raise and follow complaints, manage saved addresses, and manage their own sub-users with per-branch visibility. Customer users are strictly scoped to their own account's data.

The three tables that carry the design

  • ShipmentEvent — append-only, no update or delete grant at the database level. Columns: id, shipmentId, packageId?, eventType, occurredAt, recordedAt, branchId?, userId?, vehicleId?, driverId?, tripId?, manifestId?, lat?, lng?, deviceId?, reasonCodeId?, remarks?, attachmentId?, source, idempotencyKey (unique), payload JSONB. Indexed on (shipmentId, occurredAt) and (eventType, occurredAt).
  • ScanRecord — every physical barcode read, at package granularity. Separate from ShipmentEvent because one shipment-level event can result from seventeen package scans, and shortage detection needs the package rows.
  • GpsPing — declaratively partitioned by day, with a retention policy. Raw pings are high-volume and low-value after a few months; VehicleLocation holds the current position for fast map reads and EtaSnapshot holds the derived summaries that reports need.

Monetary columns are Decimal(14,2) handled with decimal.js — never floats. Weights are Decimal(10,3). Every table carries createdAt, updatedAt, createdById, and where relevant branchId and orgId for scope enforcement. Deletes are soft everywhere in the operational core.

B.4 State machine & event write path

A single module, src/lib/shipment/state-machine.ts, owns every legal transition as data: from, event, to, required permission, required fields, and side effects. Nothing else in the codebase may write currentStatus.

  1. Validate. Is this transition legal from the current state, does the actor hold the permission, and are the mandatory fields present?
  2. Deduplicate. Reject on a repeated idempotencyKey — the offline queue will retry, and it must be safe.
  3. Append. Write the ShipmentEvent row inside a transaction.
  4. Project. Update the denormalised currentStatus, currentBranchId, currentTripId, attemptCount, and SLA fields in the same transaction.
  5. Emit. Insert into OutboxEvent in the same transaction; a worker then fans out to notifications, webhooks, and downstream automations. Nothing external is called inside the transaction, so a dead SMS gateway can never fail a scan.

A rebuild command replays events for any shipment and asserts the projection matches — run in CI against a fixture set and available to support when a status looks wrong.

B.5 Offline-first field PWA

Field staff work in basements, industrial estates, and lifts. Offline is the normal case, not the edge case.

  • Shell and data. Service worker caches the app shell; the day's assigned tasks are pre-fetched into IndexedDB when the agent starts their shift on branch WiFi.
  • Write queue. Every action — scan, attempt, delivery, COD, photo — is written locally with a client-generated UUID as idempotencyKey and an occurredAt from the device clock, then queued. The UI confirms immediately; sync is invisible.
  • Sync. Background Sync where supported, with a foreground retry loop as fallback. Ordered per shipment, exponential backoff, never blocking the operator.
  • Clock drift. Server records the offset between device time and server time at sync; a drift beyond a threshold flags the events for review rather than silently accepting a delivery timestamped tomorrow.
  • Media. Photos are compressed client-side (long edge ~1600px, ~200KB) and uploaded separately from the event so a delivery confirms instantly and the image follows.
  • Conflict. There is none to resolve — events are additive. A late-arriving event re-runs the projection in occurredAt order.
  • Install & hardware. Add-to-home-screen prompt, full-screen display, wake lock during a run, camera and geolocation permissions requested once at onboarding.
The PWA limit, stated plainly

A PWA cannot report location while it is closed or the phone is locked. Continuous vehicle tracking therefore comes from the in-vehicle GPS device, not the agent's phone. Where you need continuous last-mile agent tracking on foot or two-wheeler, that is the specific trigger to build the native app — nothing else about the PWA plan needs to change when you do.

B.6 GPS ingestion & geofencing

A provider adapter interface keeps the core ignorant of which telematics vendor is attached:

interface GpsProvider {
  // pull-based providers
  fetchPositions(deviceIds: string[]): Promise<NormalizedPing[]>
  // push-based providers
  parseWebhook(body: unknown, signature: string): NormalizedPing[]
}

type NormalizedPing = {
  deviceId, lat, lng, speedKmph, heading,
  ignition, recordedAt, providerRef
}

The pipeline, running in the gps-ingest worker:

  1. Ingest — poll each configured provider on its own interval, or accept a signed webhook. Duplicate (deviceId, recordedAt) pairs are dropped.
  2. Persist — insert into the day's gps_ping partition; upsert vehicle_location with the latest fix.
  3. Evaluate geofences — a single PostGIS query per ping: which fences contain this point (ST_Contains for polygons, ST_DWithin on geography for circles). Compare to the vehicle's previous fence set; the difference is an enter or exit.
  4. Debounce — a fence event only fires after N consecutive pings inside or outside, so a truck idling on the boundary does not generate forty arrival events.
  5. Emit — enter at a branch fence writes a vehicle arrival, and a hub-arrival event for every shipment on the active trip. This is where most manual status updating disappears.
  6. Derive — recompute remaining distance along the planned route and ETA from a rolling average speed; check deviation from the route polyline and stationary duration; raise exceptions.

Retention: raw pings kept hot for 90 days, then downsampled to one fix per five minutes and archived. Trip replay reads the archive.

B.7 Freight engine

Rating is a pure function with no database access of its own — it receives a resolved rate-card version and a shipment snapshot, and returns priced lines plus a trace. That makes it exhaustively unit-testable, and the trace is what settles billing arguments.

calculateFreight(shipment, rateCardVersion, context) → {
  chargeableWeight,      // max(actual, volumetric), divisor from service type
  lines: [{ chargeType, basis, rate, quantity, amount, ruleId }],
  taxes: [{ head, rate, amount, isReverseCharge }],
  total,
  trace                  // every rule considered, matched or skipped, and why
}

Resolution order when several rate rules could match: customer-specific lane → customer zone pair → customer default → published tariff for the lane → published default. The first match wins and the trace records what was skipped. A shipment on a lane with no rate rule does not silently price at zero — it books with an unrated flag and appears on the rate card coverage gaps report.

FreightCalculation stores the trace against the shipment at booking and again at invoicing. When the hub revises chargeable weight, a re-rate runs, the delta is recorded, and the customer is notified if the increase exceeds the configured tolerance.
